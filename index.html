🛰 How to Reach Orbit

A first flight plan — follow these steps and you'll be circling the Earth.

  1. Build for the job. You'll want roughly 3,500–4,500 m/s of Δv and a liftoff TWR above about 1.3. Watch the Δv left and TWR numbers while you build, or just use a Quick Launch preset for your first try.
  2. Launch straight up. Full throttle (Z) and climb vertically for the first 5–10 km, clear of the thickest air.
  3. Start a gentle gravity turn. Tip a few degrees toward the horizon, then press T to switch the autopilot to Prograde — it'll keep the nose pointed along your direction of travel from here on.
  4. Keep leaning over. As you climb, gravity and drag will naturally arc your path — by the time you approach 60 km you should be flying nearly flat, parallel to the ground.
  5. Watch your Apoapsis. Keep the throttle up until the Apoapsis reading passes 70–100 km, then cut it (X). That's the highest point your current path reaches — comfortably above the 60 km "space" line.
  6. Coast to Apoapsis. Use time warp (.) to skip ahead. You're still on a suborbital arc — if you don't burn again, you'll fall back down.
  7. Circularize. Right as you crest Apoapsis, throttle up again with the autopilot on Prograde. This raises your Periapsis — the low point of the orbit. Keep burning until Periapsis also reads above 60 km.
  8. Cut the engine. With both Apoapsis and Periapsis above 60 km, you're in a stable orbit that won't decay — no more fuel needed. ★ "Orbit the Earth" unlocks the moment you get there.

Coming back down

Air that thick, hit that fast, gets hot. The Hull heat readout appears on the HUD once friction starts to bite — at 100% parts begin burning away, engines and fins first.

Come in shallowDrop Periapsis to around 10–30 km, not into the ground.
